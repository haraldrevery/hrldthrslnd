Choosing between the two

Variant B needs one photograph strong enough to carry a whole screen, and quiet enough in the top third that a headline can sit on it. Where there is no such picture, variant A is the better answer — it asks for no imagery at all.

Both are the same block underneath. Swapping one for the other is adding or removing the photographic modifier and the .hero-media layer; the height, the bottom fade, and the padding that keeps the copy clear of that fade are all in .hero-stage and do not change.

The modifier comes in two forms. This page uses .hero-stage-photo-adaptive, which washes the picture towards the page's own ground and sets the type to match, so the hero answers to the visitor's colour-scheme setting like the rest of the site. .hero-stage-photo is the other one: a fixed dark scrim and light type in both schemes, for a photograph too bright or too busy under the headline for ink to hold. Use one or the other, never both — the fixed variant pins several colours to --paper, and a hero carrying both classes comes out paper on paper in light mode.

What to check before shipping one

Look at it in both colour schemes, on a phone in landscape, and with the picture replaced by a much brighter one. The first two are where the layout gives, the third is where the scrim does — and with the adaptive modifier the light scheme is the half to check first, because near-black type needs more of the photograph taken out from under it than near-white type does.
