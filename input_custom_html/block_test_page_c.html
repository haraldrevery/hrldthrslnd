Section 02 — Wash

Fog is a
blend mode

Fog does to a floodlit pitch exactly what a scrim does to a photograph: it takes out the middle distance and leaves a foreground and a suggestion. The difference is that one of the two is honest about being an effect.

Section 03 — Field notes

Jotunheimen,
from the top

Note 01 · 2469 m

The distance goes flat

Above the last ridge every peak is the same colour. Depth stops coming from tone and starts coming from where things overlap — which is a layout problem the mountains solved first.

Note 02 · Glacier

Blue is a thickness

Glacier blue is not a pigment, it is how far the light had to travel through the ice before it came back out. The same is true of every frosted panel on this page.

Note 03 · Descent

A photograph is not the walk

Eleven hours compress to one frame, and the frame keeps the view but loses the weather. The caption is where the rest of it has to go, so the caption is worth writing.

Reading this against the real index

The front page stacks its bands: hero, then a card grid, then the subject chips. Every edge is horizontal and every section starts where the last one ended. It is easy to scan and it never surprises you.

This one overlaps instead, and pays for it. The collage needs two photographs that agree with each other and a portrait strong enough to sit under a masthead; the cluster and the staircase each need exactly six pictures and a person to decide which of them is wide; the double exposure needs one frame dark enough for another to show through it; the notes need a photograph with a quiet middle. None of that survives being generated from a folder of posts, which is why the real index is not built this way and this page is hand-written HTML.

Compare the two hero treatments as well — variant A and variant B — and every other block on the block test page.

What to check

  • Both colour schemes. The hero and the slices follow the system setting; the two photo stages deliberately do not.
  • A phone. Every overlap unwinds to a single column — nothing here is absolutely positioned.
  • Reduced motion. The gradient headlines stop animating and stay legible on the dark bands.
  • Scripting off. Only the lightbox goes; the page is otherwise complete.
  • The double exposure in section 01. The ridge should show through the dark half of the portrait and nowhere else; if the sky bleeds onto the page behind the frame, the blend has escaped its stacking context.
  • The archive on a touch screen. The frames should stop overlapping and every caption should be drawn — the lap and the hover reveal are one feature.
  • The archive with a keyboard. Tabbing to a frame should reveal its caption and lift it over its neighbours, exactly as pointing at it does.
  • The lightbox arrows. They should step through those six frames and no others.
